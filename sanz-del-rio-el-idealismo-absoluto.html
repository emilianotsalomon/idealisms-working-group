But the process by abstraction, it will be said, is an open, legitimate, universal process in science and in common life, as all History attests. What prevents, then, following this path (in which the excellence and freedom of thought shows itself) to its extreme superior point, in order to reconstruct from here all the sensible concrete, present reality, or rather, to reconceive it in entire freedom of thought and understand it?—Who denies, I answer, that the process by abstraction is legitimate and constant, and even, up to the present historical and scientific human age, the predominant one as a straight and entire process toward truth? But the process by abstraction is a process within the understanding, and a middle process relatively in separating (in our thought) the common from the concrete in order to recognize each one analytically, and to understand them afterwards as they are in reality,— the common in the individual and the individual in the common. It is not, then, the proper process nor the straight nor the entire one toward real truth; nor is it, in the object, the process of reality; nor, in us, is it the process of reason nor of the whole spirit: but, under all these respects, it is a limited, conditional, subjective and purely analytic process. It is not, therefore, a first nor last process in science nor in life but a middle, intermediate one; and in itself it is not legitimate, but illegitimate and perverting (corruptio optimi pessima), when we take it unreflectively as first and last. Nor is the most excellent thing of the spirit the subjective ideal freedom of the understanding, but the interiorly free necessity of reason over it.
