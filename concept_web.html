Concept web

Which works use a concept, and which works are closest to one another by the concepts they share, within a tradition and across traditions and languages.

How to read it

Concepts are the significant words of each text (originals and their English translations). Old spellings count as one (Theil, Teil), and a word a glossary renders in English counts as that English concept (Schauung → beholding). Words about the edition (printed, page, translation) are left out.

Similarity runs from 0 to 1: how far two works give weight to the same concepts, distinctive ones counting more than common ones (the cosine of their TF-IDF vectors). Works by the same author are usually closest; the lists of closest works in another tradition and in another language show the links that cross. Under 0.1 a likeness is faint (greyed under 0.08); a work must share at least three concepts to be listed, and a short text (a notice, a preface) counts for less.

A cross-tradition link marks a work whose closest work in another tradition is at — or more (the top sixth of the library). Year is the year of the edition in the library, which is not always the first publication.

The figures come from word use, not from reading: a link says two works share vocabulary, which is a lead to follow, not a finding.
